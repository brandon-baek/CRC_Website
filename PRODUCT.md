# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The primary users are Korean-American adults in Los Angeles, especially older adults, fraud victims, and family members helping someone through a suspicious or urgent situation. Many arrive on a phone, may prefer Korean, and may be anxious because money, identity information, or an account is at risk.

Their immediate job is to understand what happened, protect what can still be protected, and reach the correct official reporting agency without being misled by jargon or another scam.

## Product Purpose

The Consumer Resource Center provides bilingual scam education, a guided reporting-agency matcher, a plain-language government directory, and local help navigating official reporting. Success means a visitor understands the next safe action, reaches a legitimate government destination, and never mistakes CRC for a government agency, law firm, or investigative service.

## Brand Personality

Reassuring, plainspoken, community-rooted.

The interface should feel like a trusted neighborhood resource: calm under pressure, direct about risk, and warm without becoming casual about fraud. It should communicate competence through clarity rather than institutional formality.

## Anti-references

- Generic government-blue portals that feel distant or bureaucratic.
- Purple-gradient SaaS landing pages, glassmorphism, and hero metric templates.
- Identical icon-card grids that make every kind of information look the same.
- Pill-shaped controls applied to every action.
- Decorative stock imagery, fabricated staff portraits, invented testimonials, or any trust signal that is not true.
- English-first layouts that shrink Korean text until it feels secondary.

## Design Principles

1. **Protect first.** When money or accounts may be at risk, the first safe action must appear before education, video, or organizational promotion.
2. **Name the destination.** Buttons describe what happens next, such as "Find where to report," rather than vague promises of help.
3. **Earn trust with facts.** Show real contact methods, official links, organizational limits, and verifiable relationships. Remove unfinished public placeholders.
4. **Give each task its own shape.** Reporting choices, educational reading, news, and organizational information must not collapse into the same card grid.
5. **Treat Korean as primary.** Korean and English receive equal hierarchy, complete actions, and equivalent meaning.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

The baseline is WCAG 2.2 AA. Normal text must meet 4.5:1 contrast, interactive targets must be at least 44 by 44 CSS pixels, keyboard focus must always be visible, dialogs and disclosure controls must be operable without a pointer, and reduced-motion preferences must be respected. Body type starts at 18px with a maximum reading width near 68 characters. No meaning may depend on color alone. English and Korean interfaces must remain usable at narrow mobile widths and under text enlargement.
